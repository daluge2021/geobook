Per-Engine Optimization: What Each AI Engine Actually Reads

"Optimize for ChatGPT" and "optimize for Perplexity" are not the same instruction. The crawlers behave differently, the engines weight differently, and one llms.txt does not reach all of them. Per-engine optimization is the layer of GEO you apply after the baseline — not instead of it.
This article covers the real, observable differences between the major engine crawlers we track, which signals each one weights, and a prioritization method that keeps the work proportional.

The Baseline Is Still Everything

Before any engine-specific layer, the non-negotiable baseline is the same for every engine:

  • Fetchable, likeable, compressible pages answers can cite.
  • Answer-first structure with the question and answer in the first block (answer assetization).
  • Entity-annotated content with consistent internal links (entity graph).
  • A published llms.txt (guide) and stable robots.txt rules for known AI user-agents (crawler management).

This baseline gets you the first 90 percent of coverage on every engine at once. Per-engine work is about the marginal differences — and about not assuming one engine's behavior applies to another.

The pattern in our data: GPTBot gives you broad index coverage, ClaudeBot gives you disciplined sitemap-driven coverage, and PerplexityBot re-fetches on demand so freshness and plain-language accuracy matter more there. Garbage in, different garbage out.

llms.txt: The Engine-Agnostic Misunderstanding

Public datasets we track still show the same reality: only a minority of domains publish llms.txt at all, almost all of them receive zero crawler requests for it, and Google has stated its AI products are not consuming it. Publishing llms.txt stays worthwhile — it is a low-cost, standard, engine-agnostic control file (advanced guide) — but it must not be your only channel.

Two consequences for per-engine work:

  • Do not build an entire strategy on llms.txt. Verify in your server logs whether engines actually request it; many do not.
  • Do not trust llms.txt to reach every engine. Content that exists only in llms.txt is invisible to engines that ignore it. Every important answer must also exist on a rendered page.

The rule of thumb: keep every answer in one universal asset, then apply layers for distribution (robots, sitemaps, llms.txt sections) and measurement (per-engine citation counts) rather than producing separate content per engine.

Prioritization: The Three-Input Ranking

With limited budget, rank engines by three inputs, in order:

  1. Where your audience is. Not where "AI is hottest" — where your buyers ask questions. If your category research happens in Perplexity (many-source, list-heavy answers), Perplexity outranks ChatGPT for you even though ChatGPT has more users.
  2. Where you can observe a change. An engine you cannot measure is a program you cannot run (monitoring tools). Start with the engines your logs and trackers can actually split out.
  3. Where competitors already win. Use a competitive sweep (competitive AI analysis) to see whose brand already appears in each engine's answers; that is your real priority signal.

A pragmatic starting split: GPTBot and ClaudeBot first for index coverage; then PerplexityBot once you can measure its verify traffic and citation share; AI Overviews as a Google-adjacent surface you monitor regionally.

Measuring Per-Engine Performance

Split the same core indicators by engine:

  • Index coverage: per-engine pages fetched and re-fetched, in your server logs (crawler data).
  • Completeness: which sitemap sections each engine actually traverses.
  • Citation share: per-engine mentions of your brand in generated answers (citation share).
  • Answer path: zero-click presence vs brand-visit lift, per engine (attribution).

Put the split view on your dashboard (dashboard guide) so you spot one engine regressing while the others hold — that is the first sign of a per-engine control or freshness issue, not a strategy failure.

FAQ

Does GEO work the same way on every AI engine? No. Discovery, trust weighting, and llms.txt usage differ per engine. A universal baseline plus small per-engine layers is the correct scope.

Which AI crawlers should I prioritize? The engines your audience actually uses, where you can measure a change, and where competitors already win. Start with GPTBot and ClaudeBot for coverage, add PerplexityBot when measurable.

Do all AI engines read llms.txt? No. Only a minority read it and Google's AI products are not using it. Publish it as a low-cost standard control file, never as the only channel.

How do I optimize for each engine without making extra content sets? Universal answer assets plus per-engine layers on distribution, freshness, structured data, and monitoring. You optimize reach per engine; the answer stays the same.

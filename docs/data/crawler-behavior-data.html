AI Crawler Behavior Report: 12 Days of Real Crawl Data on a GEO Site

Over 12 days we logged every request to a live GEO-focused site through a Cloudflare Worker into a D1 database — 4,115 crawler hits. The data shows that Bingbot is the dominant AI gateway, ClaudeBot is the most consistent, Amazon is scanning the widest surface, and OpenAI crawls in two distinct modes.
This report is a primary-data study of how AI crawlers actually behave: crawler-by-crawler breakdown, the pages they prioritize, and what the patterns imply for Generative Engine Optimization (GEO).

How We Collected the Data

The method is straightforward: tag every inbound request by user agent, record path, HTTP status, page type and referer into a D1 database via a Cloudflare Worker, then aggregate over 12 consecutive days.

Each request that reaches the site is logged server-side. Crawlers are identified by their user agent strings (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Amazonbot, OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Bingbot, Googlebot and others). We excluded a large block of "null" user-agent traffic that largely probed sensitive paths — a scanner signature we describe separately below.

Because the site is fully server-rendered HTML with no JavaScript dependency for content, crawlers receive the same clean answer-first pages a human does. That makes the data a fair picture of how answer engines consume it.

What Do the Top Crawler Patterns Tell Us?

Each leading crawler reveals a distinct strategy worth acting on.

1. Bing is the primary AI gateway

Bingbot hit 95 unique pages and visited every single day for 12 straight days. This matters because Bing is the indexing backend behind Copilot, ChatGPT's web search and Perplexity. When you optimize for Bing, you are effectively optimizing for the largest share of AI answer traffic.

Action: Make Bing Webmaster Tools your primary AI analytics. It now reports the free citation share metric — your page's share of all citations on a topic — which Google Search Console does not expose.

2. ClaudeBot is the most predictable

ClaudeBot visited 11 of 12 days with steady 18-42 requests/day and no visible bursts. It does not need to be "submitted" to — it simply finds and re-checks content on a reliable cadence, typically indexing new pages within 24-48 hours.

Action: Publish and wait. New articles will be picked up by Claude quickly without manual pinging.

3. Amazon is scanning the widest surface

Amazonbot reached 117 unique pages — the highest page diversity of any crawler despite ranking mid-pack on total volume. This breadth suggests Amazon is building a comprehensive content index for AI shopping experiences like Alexa+.

Action: If you sell products, your pages are being evaluated by Amazon's AI. Product and schema markup is no longer optional.

4. OpenAI shows two distinct behaviors

OpenAI operates two very different crawlers. OAI-SearchBot (111 hits over 12 days) is steady and Bing-powered. ChatGPT-User (61 hits over just 4 days) is bursty and user-triggered — it spiked to 57 requests on a single day, then went quiet, which is the signature of a real user asking ChatGPT to search on demand.

Action: ChatGPT's real-time web search genuinely re-crawls the pages it cites when a user asks. Well-structured, answer-first content here directly improves the chance of a live citation.

5. Perplexity is selective but high-quality

PerplexityBot was the quietest major crawler (29 hits over 7 days) yet covered 24 unique pages. It crawls less but more deliberately, apparently prioritizing structured data and clear answers. A Perplexity citation functions like a high-authority backlink for AI surfaces.

What Sets PerplexityBot Apart From the Crowd?

Most answer engines crawl broadly and frequently. Perplexity takes a different approach: fewer total requests, higher page diversity relative to its volume, and a tendency to re-check pages that carry explicit structured data. For a site owner, this means content aimed at Perplexity should maximize question-style headings and tables — the elements that make answers immediately extractable.

Perplexity does not reward the same click-driven signals as Google. It rewards clarity, structure and standalone answerability.

Where Do External Referrers Fit Into the Data?

Only six external domains sent referral traffic in the period: google.com (18 hits), sahammurah.com (11), reddit.com (3), and a handful of singles. External mentions are a leading indicator: a single Reddit post or a link from an unfamiliar domain can trigger a cluster of new crawler visits from domains you have never seen.

This ties directly to the source authority principle — crawlers and citation engines favor entities that are independently referenced. Referral spikes from any third party are therefore more valuable than their raw volume suggests.

What Are the Practical Implications for GEO?

Start with Bing Webmaster Tools for AI data, keep every page JSON-LD structured and answer-first, publish consistently for Claude, and treat external mentions — not just rankings — as the unit of growth.
  1. Track AI via Bing, not just Google. Bing powers Copilot, ChatGPT search and Perplexity, and it is the only free source of citation-share data.
  2. Make content standalone-quotable. Answer-first paragraphs and question-style tables are extracted directly by these crawlers.
  3. Publish on a cadence Claude can index. New content is re-checked within about two days with no submission needed.
  4. Treat third-party mentions as signal. External referrers correlate with bursts of new crawler visits.
  5. Filter scanner noise. A large share of "crawler" volume can be a hostile scan using a borrowed user agent — inspect paths, not just counts.

The crawlers are already here. The question is how fast your content becomes the answer they habitually cite.

What Are Good GEO Benchmarks? Citation Rates, SOV and Q&A Share

"What's a good citation rate?" is one of the most common GEO questions, and the worst answer is a single number with a confident font. Different tools define the denominator differently, different categories have different baselines, and most published benchmarks come from the people selling the measurement.
This article separates the benchmark numbers worth keeping from the ones worth ignoring, then gives you a method to build a baseline for your niche — which is the only benchmark that actually answers your question.

Why GEO Benchmarks Are Tricky

Before any number, the static: GEO benchmarks are not like SEO benchmarks. In SEO, rank is a near-universal metric with settled definitions. In GEO, three things are still unsettled:

  • The denominator. "Citation rate" can mean your citations divided by all citations (share), divided by total answers sampled, or divided by citations plus answer carry-overs. Same brand, same data, wildly different percentages — our four-engine field test documented exactly this split.
  • The engine mix. ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews cite at very different volumes and weights, so "my rate" depends on which engine you watched.
  • The publisher incentive. Most benchmark tables are marketing. That doesn't make them wrong — it makes them directional. We label vendor-sourced values as such below.

With those caveats stated, here is the set of numbers that survives cross-checking.

Citation Rate Bands, Explained

The band structure exists because citation rate is a niche-relative number. In a small B2B category with three credible suppliers, a 15% citation rate is weak; in a crowded consumer category with forty quoted sources, 15% is excellent. Bands make the number interpretable without pretending precision:

  • Below ~8%: you are not reliably part of the answer supply. Work is still structural — crawl access, coverage of the core query set, basic authority signals.
  • ~8-15%: presence exists. This is the "we finally got cited" zone. The right reaction is to widen coverage, not to declare victory.
  • ~20-30%: traction. You now change what the category's default AI answer looks like. This is where internal stakeholders start believing, because competitors visibly lose share to you.
  • ~40%+: leadership. You are cited first and often, and marginal queries lean your way without you working them.

Use these to set internal expectations, and always measure the same denominator every month — consistency beats precision.

Share of Voice: The 15% Line

If citation rate is your share of answers, share of voice (SOV) is your share of conversational mentions — whether the brand is named, positively contextualized, or recommended. Vendors commonly publish a ~15%+ sustained SOV as the "leadership" line, and it has face validity: in most markets, the category mental default sits north of one answer in six.

SOV is also where the third-party share shows up. Independent studies repeatedly find community platforms over-represented in AI answers — Reddit alone accounts for a high-teens percentage of citations in several 2025-2026 analyses. For a commercial brand, that means your SOV ceiling depends partly on sources you do not control, which is why owned plus third-party source strategy matters: your own properties are ~86% of your addressable citation supply, but the visible competition includes platforms you cannot own.

Answer Layout and Citation Counts by Engine

Citation counts are the most misread benchmark in GEO. Independent 2026 sampling (MaximusLabs) shows Perplexity answers commonly carry roughly 20+ citations each, while ChatGPT answers average in the single digits to low double digits. This is a format difference, not a quality difference:

EngineTypical citation volumeWhat it means for your strategy
PerplexityHigh (frequently 15-25 per answer)Volume game — appearing in many answers compounds quickly; a 1-2% share is visible
ChatGPTLow (often under 10)Winner-takes-most — being in the handful of cited sources is what counts
Gemini / GoogleMedium, answer-dependentBlended with search results; entity and structured-data signals weigh heavily

We cover engine-by-engine tactics in per-engine optimization. For benchmark purposes, the takeaway is: never compare your citation count across engines; compare only within one engine, against one fixed query set.

What Not to Use Benchmarks For

  • Contractual performance guarantees. Vendor benchmarks are not a measurable KPI for your niche until you reproduce the exact denominator definition. Use them to set brackets, not SLAs.
  • Cross-industry bragging. A 30% rate in a two-supplier niche is not comparable to 30% in a crowded consumer vertical. Always benchmark within your category.
  • Timeline promises. Benchmarks describe a destination, not a speed. How fast you reach a band depends on starting authority — see our GEO timeline analysis.

How to Build Your Own Baseline

A benchmark you control beats a vendor slide every time. The method:

  1. Fix a query set. 20-50 questions your buyers actually ask, weighted to your revenue priorities. Save them verbatim; never edit the set silently.
  2. Fix a denominator. Write your formula down — for example "brand citations / total citations across all answers sampled" — and apply it identically every cycle.
  3. Sample multiple engines. Run the set in 2-3 engines you care about. Record separately; score yourself and 3-5 competitors on the same questions.
  4. Measure monthly, report on trajectory. Given ~40-60% monthly answer drift, a single snapshot is noise. Your trend line over 3-4 months is the real benchmark.
  5. Anchor to revenue questions. A high citation rate on questions nobody asks is a vanity metric. Weight your baseline by commercial importance and fold it into the core metrics system.

Six months of clean monthly sampling will tell you more about "good for your niche" than any industry table — and it is the same discipline behind a functional GEO data dashboard.

FAQ

What is a good citation rate for GEO? Directional B2B bands: 8-15% is minimal presence, 20-30% is traction, and 40%+ is leader-level. Definitions differ between tools, so keep your own denominator fixed.

What share of voice makes you an AI category leader? A commonly cited heuristic is sustained SOV of 15%+ across priority topics. Your real target should be a growing advantage over the next competitor, not a fixed number.

How many citations do AI answers typically show? Independent sampling reports Perplexity answers averaging around 20+ citations and ChatGPT answers averaging under 10. Compare within one engine only.

Should I trust vendor-published GEO benchmarks? As directional references, yes; as targets, no. Build a fixed-query, fixed-formula baseline and measure movement over at least three months.

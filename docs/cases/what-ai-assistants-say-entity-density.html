What AI Assistants Say About Entity Density — And How Claude Cited This Site

Suppose your team spends weeks building a page about a specific GEO concept — defining it, linking it to ecosystem terms, and backing it with research data.
Then you ask four AI engines what that concept means.
Three of them say "the term isn't standardized." One of them, after being shown your page, repeats your definition back nearly word-for-word — and links to you as the source.
That is exactly what happened to this site's entity association density page in a September 2026 field test.

Entity Association Density — the measure of how interconnected the entities referenced in your content are — is real, but it is not yet a standard industry metric. ChatGPT, Claude and Grok all reported the same finding: no canonical definition exists in GEO, SEO, or knowledge-graph literature. Yet the concept has powerful close cousins, and one engine's response turned out to be a live demonstration of this site's own thesis about why AI extracts and cites content.

The Surprising Consensus: "This Term Isn't Standardized"

Asked head-on, three engines gave essentially the same caveat:

EngineFirst responseClosest concept offered
ChatGPTNot a universally standardized metric like citation shareKeyword density vs. entity association: "how many meaningful, consistent relationships does AI see between my entity and the concepts I want to be known for?"
ClaudeCouldn't find a standardized, widely-recognized term with a single canonical sourceEntity density, relationship richness, and GEO Lab's unique-entities-per-1,000-words definition
GrokNo widely established definition; no matching technical definition foundBrand association density (2019 JPBM paper) and entity density (The GEO Lab)
PerplexityTreats it as an emerging GEO/AEO metric with a published definitionBrand-attribute co-mention density; related to entity salience and corroboration density

Claude and Grok both went a step further and explained why the phrase looks familiar: entity density (the count of unique named entities per 1,000 words) is measurable and studied, while the "association" layer — how explicitly those entities are connected to each other — comes from knowledge-graph research on relationship richness rather than from SEO standards.

The Definition Gap Is an Opportunity

Every generic definition of a new metric starts on someone's page before it becomes an industry standard. The fact that engines describe this concept inconsistently is not evidence that it is useless — it is evidence that whoever defines it clearly, backs it with data, and gets cited first will shape how AI explains it to everyone else.

The Close Cousins Every GEO Practitioner Should Know

Entity Density: Countable and Tested

The GEO Lab experiments define entity density as unique entities per 1,000 words, measured with a named-entity model over a filtered label set — and importantly, their tests show higher density improves extractability and citation likelihood within an already citation-eligible domain, but does not overcome domain-authority gates on competitive queries. That echoes the advice in our entity density article: focus on associations around a strong, already-trusted brand position.

The Two-Layer Way to Think About It

ChatGPT's answer was the most useful framing of the distinction:

"Keyword density asks 'how many times did I use the word?' Entity association density asks 'how many meaningful, consistent relationships does the AI/search system see between my entity and the concepts I want to be known for?'"

Concretely: naming "CRM" ten times is keyword density. Interconnecting "CRM → Salesforce → marketing automation → data privacy → ROI measurement → B2B SaaS" across your content, schema, and links is entity association density. AI engines extract multiple related facts from a connected passage — which is why the higher entropy of a tightly woven page lowers the effort for retrieval.

How Claude Ended Up Citing This Site

The interesting part happened after the first answers. Claude had said it couldn't find a definition. It was then given the URL of this site's Entity Association Density & Content Cross-Verification page. Claude's second response reproduced the page's definition nearly verbatim:

"Entity Association Density measures the extent to which the various professional terms, concepts, people, brands, technologies, and other 'entities' mentioned in your content are interconnected and cross-referenced — essentially, whether an article presents a single isolated concept or weaves a connected 'web' of related knowledge around it." — reproduced by Claude from geo010.com/metrics/entity-density.html

Claude also pulled the page's supporting Yext statistic (86% of AI citations come from authoritative, brand-managed sources; brand-owned websites alone account for 44%), passed along the page's improvement checklist, and linked back to the source page with its update date.

Why This Matters for Your GEO Work

This response is a working example of the retrieval behavior this site's article set is designed for:

  • The page's first answer block states the definition directly, so it is extractable in one passage — no paraphrasing required.
  • The definition names concrete entities (CRM, Salesforce, HubSpot, ERP) that give AI multiple connection points.
  • The supporting data (Yext's 6.8-million-citation study) gives the claim a verifiable anchor.

One caveat is worth being honest about: Claude also noted that this appears to be "a niche/independent GEO encyclopedia site rather than an established industry standard... 'entity association density' as a named metric seems to be this site's own framing." Getting cited is only step one. Converting a definition into a standard requires third-party corroboration — exactly the content cross-verification behavior the same page teaches.

What This Tells Us About Emerging GEO Terminology

Three practical lessons for anyone building content around a young concept:

  1. Define it before the engines do. A clear first-sentence definition in an answer block is the most extractable asset for retrieval engines.
  2. Anchor it in measurable data. "Unique entities per 1,000 words" and Yext's 86% figure are what make a definition citable instead of opinion.
  3. Expect the farm-out: engines will quote you, then flag you as a niche source. That is the normal first stage of terminology diffusion — push cross-verification, glossary coverage, and third-party onward links to advance it.

Method note: Field test conducted in September 2026. Each engine was asked "What is entity association density?" through its public web interface. Claude's follow-up answer was produced after being given the URL of this site's entity density article and arrived with an explicit source link to geo010.com/metrics/entity-density.html. Engine responses were summarized for length.

What AI Assistants Say About Citation Share — A Four-Engine Field Test

Suppose you're running a GEO program. Your north-star KPI is citation share.
But one question keeps nagging you: how do the AI engines themselves define the metric you are being measured on?
In September 2026, we asked ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity and Grok the same question — "What is citation share in GEO?" — and compared their answers.
Here is what the engines agree on, where they diverge, and what that means for how you should measure.

Citation Share measures how often AI answer engines cite your domain as the evidence behind their answers, on a defined set of queries. All four engines use the same core picture: it is the AI-era equivalent of "share of voice," but measured at the source-link level. Yet when you dig into the formulas, the denominators, and the examples, the four engines split into two camps — and that split has real consequences for your GEO dashboard.

What All Four Engines Agree On

Stripping away the wording differences, the four answers converge on five points:

  • The shift from links to answers. AI engines collapse the classic "10 blue links" into a single synthesized answer with a limited set of cited sources. Being cited (or recommended) is increasingly the difference between visibility and invisibility.
  • The formula shape. Citation Share = your citations ÷ total citations across all sources for the same query set × 100. Every engine presented this shape, though the denominator varied (see below).
  • Mention is not a citation. A mention is your brand name appearing in prose; a citation is the answer explicitly relying on your content as supporting evidence. All four treat citations as the stronger authority signal.
  • It is competitive and relative. A high absolute citation rate can still mean a low competitive share if your rivals are cited far more often.
  • No universal standard exists yet. Different tools use different query sets, engines, and weighting. Every engine explicitly warned against comparing absolute percentages across platforms.

The Shared Core Definition

Perplexity gave what is arguably the cleanest consensus statement:

"Citation share in GEO is the percentage of AI-generated answers on a defined set of queries that cite your domain as a source, measured against all citations across competitors for those same queries."

Trend Beats Absolute Value

ChatGPT made the practical recommendation that all four engines repeated in some form:

"There is no universal Citation Share standard yet... 'Citation Share increased from 8% to 17% over three months' is much more meaningful than simply saying 'our Citation Share is 17%.'"

For your GEO program, the actionable takeaway is: lock one methodology, run it monthly, and watch the direction — not the headline number.

Where the Four Engines Diverge

Under the surface, the engines split into two camps on the denominator:

EngineDenominator usedEmphasized distinction
ChatGPTPercentage of answers citing you (e.g., 25 of 100 queries = 25%)Mention vs. citation; measurement funnel
ClaudeYour citations ÷ total citations across all sourcesSentiment, position, and query coverage as companion metrics
PerplexityYour citation instances ÷ total citation instances (not unique sources)Citation rate vs. citation share; per-engine tracking
GrokBoth: share of total citations vs. share of responses citing youC-SOV terminology; low ranking→citation overlap

The Two Formulas in Practice

Grok laid out the two formulations that explain most industry disagreement:

  • Share of total citations (the competitive version): your citations ÷ total citations from all domains on the query set.
  • Share of responses that cite you (the absolute version): AI responses citing you ÷ total responses sampled.

The two numbers can differ sharply. ChatGPT's example was 25 of 100 answers citing you = 25%. Grok's example was 4 citations out of 80 total citations on 10 queries = 5%. Perplexity's was 35 of 250 citation instances = 14%. Same metric name, three different denominators producing three different-looking results.

Why This Matters for Your Dashboard

If your tool reports "citation share of 14%" and a colleague's tool reports "18%," you may both be correct — with different denominators. That is why the practical standard, repeated by every engine, is consistency over precision: fix your query set, fix your denominator, track the trend.

The Data Points SEOs Should Not Miss

Two insights buried in the answers are worth lifting out:

Rankings Correlate Only Weakly with AI Citations

Grok's answer cited studies finding only ~12% overlap between top-10 Google results and AI-cited URLs. If your team is still equating "ranking well" with "being cited by AI," that overlap is your evidence that GEO is a separate game.

Platforms Are Standardizing the Metric Themselves

Three of the four engines independently mentioned the same platform feature:

  • Bing Webmaster Tools introduced an AI Citation Share metric showing your percentage of citations per grounding query.
  • Bing also provides an AI Performance report showing which URLs are cited and the grounding queries behind them.

The metric is moving from "agency vocabulary" to a first-party product feature — the best reason to start measuring now.

Implications for Your GEO Program

Build a Measurement Funnel, Not a Single Number

ChatGPT's answer was the most explicit on this. Citation share should not be monitored alone. Track this sequence:

StageQuestion it answers
AI VisibilityDoes AI see your content?
Mention ShareDoes AI talk about you?
Citation ShareDoes AI rely on you as a source?
Recommendation RateDoes AI point users to you?
AI Referral Traffic & ConversionsDoes AI users become your traffic?

Read this funnel backwards and it becomes a diagnosis tool: low citations usually trace back to low visibility or low mention rate — and those point to different fixes than "write more content."

Reconcile with Our Own Definition

GEO Encyclopedia's definition of citation share (your citations ÷ total citations on a topic) matches the competitive version that Grok, Perplexity and Claude described. The practical difference is measurement scope: we recommend tracking it together with semantic coverage — the process metric that predicts whether citation share will move.

Method note: This field test was conducted in September 2026 by asking each engine the same question through its public web interface and comparing the responses. Engine answers were summarized for length; the underlying industry data points (the ~12% ranking-citation overlap, Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Citation Share and AI Performance features) trace back to the sources cited in each engine's response and to the platform documentation linked in the measuring tools section.
